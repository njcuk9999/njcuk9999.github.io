Since September 2021, I have been a researcher at the University of Montreal with the Institute for Research on Exoplanets (iREx). I collaborate closely with the SPIRou and NIRPS teams—working with Étienne Artigau on data pipelines—and with Loïc Albert and the iREx JWST team on software for JWST.

Previously, I was a Trottier Postdoctoral Fellow at UdeM from 2017 to 2021. Before moving to Montreal, I completed a short postdoc at York University in Toronto with Ray Jayawardhana, using Gaia and SuperWASP data to study very low-mass and ultracool members of nearby young moving groups. I earned my PhD at the University of Hertfordshire (UK) in August 2016, where my thesis focused on finding ultracool dwarf and giant exoplanet companions to M dwarfs—including building a massive M dwarf catalogue and developing a new approach to identify unresolved companions.

My research focuses on all things cool: M dwarfs, brown dwarfs, and especially exoplanets, with a growing emphasis on detecting habitable worlds around cool stars. I'm also an avid programmer and love tackling any astrophysical coding challenge.
